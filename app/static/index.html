How well it does

There is no single accuracy number

A T20 innings ends near 170; a Test innings can pass 500. An average error of 30 runs would be poor in one and excellent in the other, so quoting one figure across all of them would flatter the short formats and libel the long ones. Read each row on its own.

Three things worth knowing

Why this is not the usual score predictor

Every format, one model

Most score predictors handle T20 only. This one covers T20, ODI and multi-day cricket together, by giving the model how far through the innings it is rather than how many overs have gone. A T20 at ball 60 and an ODI at ball 150 are both halfway, and the model can see that. Multi-day innings have no ball limit at all, so they take their own branch.

Nothing is scored against its own future

Every ball of one innings shares the same answer, so splitting the data at random lets the model memorise instead of predict. It is trained on the past and tested on matches played later, and every piece of context — a ground's par score, a side's recent form — counts only matches that had already happened. The results page shows what that honesty costs.

The range is checked, not claimed

Every projection comes with an 80% band. That band is calibrated against held-out matches and then measured: it currently contains the true final score 00% of the time against a target of 00%. A stated interval nobody checked is worth nothing.
